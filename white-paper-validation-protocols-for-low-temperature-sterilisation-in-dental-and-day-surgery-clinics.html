Validation Protocols for Low-Temperature Sterilisation in Dental and Day Surgery Clinics

Introduction

This paper sets out validation, routine monitoring and maintenance requirements for low temperature sterilisers in dental and day surgery clinics: vaporised hydrogen peroxide (VH2O2) systems, including gas plasma systems, ethylene oxide (EO) systems and ozone systems. It applies AS 5369:2023 and the process standards it calls up.

Scope

Installation, operational and performance qualification (IQ, OQ and PQ) for each modality, routine monitoring and load release, maintenance and requalification, and operator checklists. Device and packaging compatibility follow the device and steriliser instructions for use (IFU).

Overview of Low-Temperature Sterilisation Technologies

The following table summarises typical process characteristics. The IFU of the specific steriliser governs.

Process Temperature Cycle Time
VH2O2Less than 50 deg C [122 deg F]28 to 75 min
EO37 to 55 deg C [99 to 131 deg F]1 to 6 h exposure, plus aeration
Ozone30 to 35 deg C [86 to 95 deg F]About 4 h 15 min

Vaporised Hydrogen Peroxide: Hydrogen peroxide vapour, with a plasma phase in gas plasma systems, inactivates microorganisms by oxidation. By-products are water vapour and oxygen, so no aeration is required. Cellulose (paper), linen and liquids cannot be processed. Package in polypropylene wraps, polyolefin pouches or containers specified in the IFU. Moisture on the load prevents the cycle reaching vacuum and aborts it, so dry devices fully. Lumen length and diameter are limited to the values stated in the IFU.

Ethylene Oxide: EO inactivates microorganisms by alkylation of protein, DNA and RNA. It penetrates packaging and lumens and is compatible with most materials, but requires aeration to reduce residuals to the limits of ISO 10993-7. Mechanical aeration typically takes 8 to 12 h at 50 to 60 deg C [122 to 140 deg F]. AS 5369:2023 gives common ranges of 400 to 800 mg/L EO concentration and 50 to 90 % relative humidity at the temperature listed, with gas dwell time determined during PQ. Dry devices fully, because water reacts with EO to form ethylene glycol. Do not unload devices until the in-chamber aeration phase is complete. Where the gas supply is not self-contained within the chamber, provide a means to detect gas leaks.

Ozone: Ozone generated from oxygen, with humidification, inactivates microorganisms by oxidation and is catalytically converted to oxygen at the end of the cycle, so no aeration is required. The temperature and cycle time listed are those of a system cleared in the United States in 2003. No method-specific ISO standard exists. Before selecting an ozone system, confirm current ARTG inclusion, consumable supply and service support.

Standards and Compliance Framework in Australia

AS 5369:2023: AS 5369:2023 supersedes AS/NZS 4187:2014 and AS/NZS 4815:2006 and applies to hospital and office-based settings. Low temperature sterilisation systems must be tested for efficacy to ISO 14937, ISO 25424 for low temperature steam formaldehyde or ISO 22441 for VH2O2, and must have comprehensive IFU. All processes must be validated, and PQ must demonstrate a sterility assurance level (SAL) of 1 in 1,000,000 for terminally sterilised devices.

Process and Equipment Standards: Conduct PQ to ISO 22441 for VH2O2, ISO 11135 for EO (ISO 14937 for flexible chamber EO processes) and ISO 14937 where no other standard applies, including ozone. EO sterilisers conform to EN 1422 and ISO/TS 22421, and low temperature hydrogen peroxide sterilisers to ISO 22441 and ISO/TS 22421.

Office-Based Practices: Where calibration, OQ and PQ cannot be conducted on site, e.g. some remote office-based practices, off-site validation is permitted. The service provider uses devices and loading configurations representative of the facility. On return, review the test report, conduct the steriliser performance tests and process reference loads with biological and chemical indicators before patient use.

Therapeutic Goods: Sterilisers supplied in Australia must be included in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG). Record the ARTG entry at IQ.

Work Health and Safety: Safe Work Australia classifies EO as a non-threshold genotoxic carcinogen. From 1 December 2026 it has no workplace exposure limit, so eliminate or minimise exposure so far as reasonably practicable. The hydrogen peroxide exposure standard is 1 ppm TWA, reducing to a workplace exposure limit of 0.5 ppm TWA from 1 December 2026. The ozone exposure standard is a peak limitation of 0.1 ppm. EO is flammable. Store sterilant cassettes and cartridges as specified in the Safety Data Sheet.

Infection Control Guidelines: Reprocessing policies also follow the NHMRC Australian Guidelines for the Prevention and Control of Infection in Healthcare.

Qualification Testing: Installation, Operational, and Performance Qualification

Perform IQ and OQ for each steriliser and PQ for each process delivered by each steriliser, under a documented validation protocol that identifies the equipment and any ancillary equipment such as aeration cabinets. Confirm the calibration status of all instrumentation, including test instruments, immediately before IQ, OQ and PQ. Record all tests and results.

Installation Qualification (IQ)

IQ demonstrates that the steriliser and ancillary items are supplied and installed to their specification. Before installation, specify the location, confirm that environmental conditions and services meet the equipment specification, and obtain the specification, calibration documentation and operating instructions.

  • Location and Environment: Room temperature, humidity and ventilation within IFU limits. For EO, exhaust to outside or to catalytic abatement, and an EO room monitor installed.

  • Services: Electrical supply voltage, phase and protection, compressed air, oxygen for ozone generation, water and drainage to the IFU.

  • Safety Systems: Door interlock prevents cycle start with the door open. Emergency stop, alarms and EO leak detection function.

  • Documentation: Model, serial number, ARTG entry, IFU, calibration certificates, maintenance schedule and operator training records.

  • Sign-Off: A competent person signs the IQ report.

Operational Qualification (OQ)

OQ demonstrates, with the chamber empty or with test materials, that the steriliser delivers the cycles defined in its specification. Perform OQ after installation or relocation, when a service changes, when the steriliser is modified to deliver a new process, when new devices or loading configurations are introduced, and after repair before return to service.

  • Calibration: Verify temperature, pressure, humidity and sterilant concentration or dose sensors against calibrated references.

  • Cycle Tests: Run each cycle type and confirm from the cycle record that every phase, including vacuum, conditioning, sterilant injection, exposure, plasma or conversion and aeration, reaches its set points.

  • Fault Response: Simulate faults, e.g. failure to reach vacuum, low sterilant concentration, door open and power failure. Confirm the steriliser aborts and alarms, and that a failed cycle is not recorded as successful.

  • Leak Test: Run the leak or vacuum test specified in the IFU and record the result against its limit.

  • Records: Confirm the printer or electronic record captures every cycle.

  • Acceptance: Compare results with acceptance criteria predefined from the IFU and the applicable standard. Resolve deviations before PQ.

Performance Qualification (PQ)

PQ uses a load representative of routine loads and based on the most challenging load. Specify and document the total load mass, package devices as in routine use and document device orientation. PQ comprises physical PQ, which confirms critical parameters such as exposure time and sterilant concentration within the load, and microbiological PQ, which places biological indicators (BIs) where sterilising conditions are most difficult to achieve. Perform PQ after IQ and OQ for new or relocated equipment, after repairs or service changes that can affect device quality, when the steriliser is modified for a new process, and when new devices, packaging or loading configurations are introduced unless equivalence to a qualified reference load is demonstrated.

  • Worst-Case Load: Include the longest and narrowest lumens within IFU limits, the densest sets and the maximum load mass.

  • Biological Indicators: Use BIs conforming to the applicable part of ISO 11138, documented as more resistant than any residual bioburden, at the positions most difficult to sterilise or within a process challenge device (PCD). VH2O2 BIs commonly use Geobacillus stearothermophilus and EO BIs Bacillus atrophaeus. EO PCDs conform to ISO 11135.

  • Chemical Indicators: Place internal chemical indicators (CIs) conforming to the applicable part of ISO 11140 beside the BIs. CIs are not the sole means of establishing the process.

  • Cycles: Complete at least three consecutive successful exposures within defined tolerances. Review and correct any exposure outside tolerance before starting a new series. Document a failure caused by factors unrelated to the process, e.g. power failure, as unrelated, and continue the series.

  • Acceptance: All BIs show no growth, all CIs pass, all physical parameters are within the validated range, and packs are intact and dry. For EO, confirm that the validated aeration achieves ISO 10993-7 residual limits for the devices processed.

  • Report: A competent person approves the validation report covering IQ, OQ and PQ before release to routine use. Requalify annually.

Load Release: Release a load only when the cycle record confirms the process parameters established during PQ, external CIs show the specified change, packs are intact with no visible moisture, and BI and PCD results are correct where used. For EO, BIs show no growth and records confirm any external aeration was performed to the process specification. Quarantine loads that fail any criterion.

BI Failure: Remove the steriliser from service, recall loads processed since the last negative BI, investigate, correct the cause and requalify before return to use.

Trend Data: Trend leak rates, vacuum times, sterilant consumption, aborted cycles and indicator failures for each steriliser. Review records at defined intervals, e.g. quarterly.

Maintenance Schedules and Best Practices for Compliance

Daily: Confirm the sterilant cassette or cartridge is loaded and in date, the cycle record is functioning, and the chamber and door seal are clean and undamaged. For EO, confirm exhaust and room monitoring are operating. For ozone, confirm oxygen supply.

Periodic: Replace filters, vacuum pump oil, door seals and catalytic converters, including hydrogen peroxide and ozone destruct units and EO abators, at the intervals in the IFU. Clean the chamber by the method and frequency specified, and record it. Use only sterilants, indicators and parts specified in the IFU.

Annual: A competent person performs planned preventive maintenance to documented procedures, recalibration, an electrical safety check and requalification. The maximum interval between recalibrations is 12 months. Calibrate room gas monitors to their IFU.

Return to Use: Do not reprocess devices until specified maintenance is complete and documented. Specify the maximum permitted delay for scheduled maintenance, e.g. 10 working days after the due date. Remove the steriliser from use immediately where a fault can affect device quality or safety, or operator safety. After repair, test against the IQ and OQ specifications, and requalify where the repair can affect process efficacy.

Maintenance Records: Record the reason, date, model and serial number, location, work performed, parts replaced, the person or company performing the work and the person releasing the steriliser to use.

Downtime and Idle Periods: Where a practice has one low temperature steriliser, arrange loan equipment, sufficient instrument stock or off-site processing for maintenance and repair periods. Follow the IFU shutdown procedure for idle periods. Before return to service, confirm that maintenance and requalification are current and process a load with a BI.

Operational Guidance and Validation Checklists

Before Each Cycle:

  • Confirm devices are clean and fully dry, and lumens are within the IFU dimension limits.

  • Package in materials specified in the IFU, with no cellulose or linen for VH2O2.

  • Load to the validated configuration without exceeding the validated load mass or obstructing sterilant flow.

  • Apply an external CI to each package. Include internal CIs per facility policy, and a BI or PCD when scheduled and in every EO load.

After Each Cycle:

  • Review the cycle record against the validated parameters. Quarantine the load on any alarm or deviation.

  • Check external CIs, pack integrity and the absence of visible moisture.

  • For EO, confirm aeration is complete before unloading or release.

  • An authorised person releases the load against the release criteria and records the release.

Periodic:

  • Run and log BIs and leak tests at the scheduled frequency.

  • Test room gas monitor alarms at the frequency in their IFU.

  • Review cycle, indicator and maintenance records, and train operators on loading, release and emergency response.

Conclusion

Validate each low temperature steriliser by IQ and OQ, and each process by PQ to ISO 22441, ISO 11135 or ISO 14937, with at least three consecutive successful exposures demonstrating an SAL of 1 in 1,000,000. Check the process record each cycle, use external CIs on each package, use BIs in every EO load and at the IFU frequency for other systems, and release loads against defined criteria. Maintain to the IFU, recalibrate at intervals not exceeding 12 months and requalify annually and after repairs that can affect efficacy. Control EO, hydrogen peroxide and ozone exposure to Australian work health and safety requirements.
