Decontamination Strategies for Complex Instruments and Endoscopes in High-Volume Australian Centres

Introduction

This paper sets out the requirements and design practice for decontaminating complex surgical instruments and flexible endoscopes in high-volume Australian central sterilising services departments (CSSDs). It covers the regulatory framework, validation and routine monitoring, process stages, equipment and services selection, staff competency, and technology for throughput and traceability. Requirements are taken from AS 5369:2023 unless another source is named.

Regulatory and Standards Framework

AS 5369:2023, Reprocessing of reusable medical devices and other devices in health and non-health related facilities, was published on 15 December 2023. It supersedes AS/NZS 4187:2014 and AS/NZS 4815:2006 and applies to hospitals, office-based practices and non-health facilities that reprocess reusable medical devices (RMDs). It sets minimum requirements and calls up ISO and EN standards for equipment and process detail, so equipment and process specifications cite those standards directly.

Health service organisations demonstrate compliance through Action 3.17 of the National Safety and Quality Health Service (NSQHS) Preventing and Controlling Infections Standard. Action 3.17 requires reprocessing consistent with national and international standards and manufacturers’ instructions, a traceability process for critical and semi-critical devices capable of identifying the patient, the procedure and the devices used, and processes to plan and manage reprocessing requirements. Advisory AS24/01 from the Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care required a gap analysis against AS 5369:2023 by 30 June 2025 and a compliance plan with defined timeframes. Action 3.17 is rated not met where the gap analysis is incomplete or the plan is not being implemented and monitored.

AS 5369 requires each device to be classified using the Spaulding scheme, with the following processing hierarchy. Critical devices, which enter the vascular system or normally sterile areas of the body, are sterilised by a validated moist heat process between patients, or by a validated low-temperature process where the device cannot tolerate moist heat. Semi-critical devices, which contact mucous membranes or non-intact skin, are also sterilised by moist heat or a low-temperature process where compatible. Where sterilisation is not compatible, they receive thermal disinfection, and where thermal disinfection is not compatible, high-level disinfection (HLD). Non-critical devices, which contact intact skin only, receive low or intermediate-level disinfection where facility policy or the device instructions for use (IFU) require it. A flexible endoscope is therefore processed by HLD only when it cannot tolerate a validated sterilisation or thermal disinfection process.

The Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) regulates RMDs, their accessories and reprocessing equipment as medical devices, and AS 5369 requires these items to be included on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG). Cleaning agents are Class I medical devices. Instrument-grade disinfectants, HLD systems and liquid chemical sterilants are Class IIb. Hospital-grade disinfectants are not used on RMDs. Used devices labelled for single use are not reprocessed or reused.

Supporting documents are the Gastroenterological Society of Australia (GESA) and Gastroenterological Nurses College of Australia (GENCA) guideline Infection Prevention and Control in Endoscopy (2021, updated May 2025), which AS 5369 calls up for endoscope microbiological surveillance, the Australasian Health Facility Guidelines (AusHFG) Part B Sterilizing Services Unit (0190) and Part D, and AS 1668.2 for ventilation. Where reprocessing is outsourced, the contract defines the responsibilities of each party and requires conformance with AS 5369.

Validation and Routine Monitoring

Every cleaning, disinfection, packaging and sterilisation process is validated by installation qualification (IQ), operational qualification (OQ) and performance qualification (PQ). IQ and OQ are performed on each item of equipment. PQ is performed for each process delivered by each item of equipment. Each process has a validation protocol and a validation report approved by a competent person. Confirm the calibration status of all fitted and test instrumentation immediately before IQ, OQ and PQ.

  • Installation Qualification (IQ): Confirms that the equipment, ancillary items, services and environment conform to the installation specification. Define water, steam, compressed air, drainage, electrical and ventilation requirements before purchase, and test the supplied water quality before installation.

  • Operational Qualification (OQ): Demonstrates that the equipment delivers its defined process, empty or with test materials, including heat distribution studies where the equipment standard requires them. Repeat OQ after installation or relocation, after a change to a service, after modification to deliver a new process, when new devices or load configurations are introduced, and after repair before return to service.

  • Performance Qualification (PQ): Demonstrates that the process consistently yields clean, disinfected or sterile product, using devices representative of the most difficult to process in the facility. For cleaning, use an objective method in addition to visual inspection, e.g. protein residue testing using ISO 15883-5 methods. For sterilisation, demonstrate a sterility assurance level (SAL) of one in one million by concurrent physical PQ (time at temperature at the slowest-to-heat positions) and microbiological PQ (biological indicators (BIs) to ISO 11138 at the positions most difficult to sterilise). Use at least two BIs in chambers of up to 0.3 cubic metres [10.6 cubic feet], three or more in larger chambers, and additional BIs for complex loads. Complete at least three consecutive successful exposures, record total load mass and device orientation, and add the measured equilibration time to the holding time.

Requalify each process annually. Repeat PQ when a repair or service change adversely affects the process, when equipment is modified, and when new devices, packaging or load configurations are introduced, unless equivalence to a qualified reference load or product family is demonstrated.

Check the process record at the end of every cycle against the validated limits before release. For steam sterilisers with vacuum air removal, perform a daily air removal and steam penetration (Bowie and Dick type) test, and a weekly leak rate and air detector function test where an air detector is fitted. Where no air detector is fitted, perform the leak rate test daily before the Bowie and Dick type test. Release moist heat loads on the cycle record, external chemical indicator (CI) colour change, package integrity and absence of visible moisture. Use BIs in moist heat processes at a frequency set by the facility. Place a BI in every ethylene oxide load and hold release until the result is known.

For instrument washer-disinfectors (WDs), confirm temperature, holding time and detergent dosing on every cycle, verify dosing volumetrically each quarter, and test cleaning efficacy daily and quarterly. Test supply water hardness and chloride, and final rinse conductivity and total viable count (TVC), monthly, and final rinse endotoxin annually. Increase test frequency when a result is out of specification. Perform thermometric testing, load dryness, thermal disinfection efficacy and spray system checks annually. For endoscope WDs, confirm disinfectant dosing on every cycle and test final rinse water at the point where the endoscope connects, monthly for TVC, Pseudomonas aeruginosa and atypical mycobacteria and annually for endotoxin. Test ultrasonic cleaners daily to AS 2773 and check drying cabinet temperature daily. Test all reprocessing equipment for electrical safety annually.

Retain records for every cycle: equipment qualification reports, cycle records, indicator results, maintenance records and nonconformance reports. Quarantine any device that does not meet its release criteria and manage it under the nonconforming product procedure. Where a sterilisation or disinfection failure is identified after release, use the traceability system to recall affected devices and identify the patients concerned.

Reprocessing Stages for High-Throughput Facilities

Each stage is completed within a defined time and capacity, and no step is bypassed. AS 5369 requires a process map or flow diagram that manages cross-contamination risk, including airflow, with unidirectional flow from dirty to clean activities. Throughput is set by the slowest stage, so run sinks, WDs and sterilisers in parallel, stagger cycle start times, and allocate dedicated staff and bench space to loan sets and complex devices so they do not block routine flow.

  • Point-of-Use Pre-Treatment: Remove gross soil at the point of use and keep soil moist, using methods that do not damage the device or compromise later processes. Specify the maximum interval between use and the start of cleaning, and the corrective action when it is exceeded. For flexible endoscopes, aspirate and flush the working channel immediately after the procedure while the endoscope is still connected to the processor, and wipe external surfaces with a single-use low-lint cloth and detergent solution.

  • Containment and Transport: Transport used devices in closed, labelled containers or carts that protect staff and the environment. Keep dirty and clean routes separate, using dedicated lifts where volumes justify them.

  • Sorting and Disassembly: Disassemble devices to the IFU before pre-treatment or cleaning, and allocate each device to its validated cleaning pathway: WD only, manual cleaning or ultrasonic pre-treatment followed by a WD, or manual cleaning only where the IFU requires it. Use automated cleaning wherever the IFU permits, because it is more reproducible than manual cleaning. Identify lumened devices for flushing. Leak test flexible endoscopes before immersion.

  • Manual and Ultrasonic Cleaning: Provide dedicated dirty and clean sinks deep enough for full immersion, height-adjustable where possible, with water flushing of lumens on the dirty side and air flushing on the clean side. Use cleaning sinks for no other purpose. Prepare fresh detergent solution for each cleaning episode at the IFU dilution, clean below the water line to limit aerosols, and brush and flush all lumens and valves. Clean and thermally disinfect or sterilise brushes at the end of each session and at least daily. Ultrasonic cleaning only loosens soil, so follow it with a complete manual or WD cleaning process. Change ultrasonic tank solution daily and whenever soil is visible.

  • Automated Cleaning and Thermal Disinfection: Load WDs so that all surfaces and lumens are exposed, connecting lumened devices to the irrigation ports on the load carrier. Thermal disinfection to ISO 15883 achieves an A0 of 600 at a surface temperature of 80 deg C [176 deg F] held for 10 min or 90 deg C [194 deg F] held for 1 min. Unload so that cleaned devices are not recontaminated.

  • High-Level Disinfection of Endoscopes: Perform HLD to the endoscope and disinfectant IFU, in a WD conforming to ISO 15883-4 where automated. AS 5369 notes that industry guidelines may require automated disinfection, and an automated flexible endoscope reprocessor (AFER) provides a reproducible process and a cycle record for traceability. Rinse endoscopes used in sterile cavities, in immunocompromised patients or for invasive procedures, e.g. endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) and bronchoscopy, with sterile water or water filtered through a 0.22 micrometre [8.7 microinch] sterilising-grade filter. Where manual HLD is used, check the minimum recommended concentration (MRC) before each use or at least daily, and record temperature, contact time and rinse volume for each episode. Do not store devices in disinfectant. Maintain a contingency plan for AFER breakdown.

  • Drying: Dry until no residual water is visible on or in the device. Do not leave devices to dry in ambient air. Use drying cabinets conforming to AS 5330, or low-lint cloths and instrument-grade compressed air with documented safety controls. Use alcohol as a drying agent only where the IFU specifies it. Dry and store flexible endoscopes in controlled-environment storage cabinets (CESCs) conforming to EN 16442, qualified to EN 16442. Do not exceed the maximum storage time validated for the cabinet, record storage time for each endoscope, and set in the facility procedure a maximum interval between removal from the cabinet and use (e.g. 12 h), after which the endoscope is reprocessed.

  • Inspection, Assembly and Packaging: Inspect each device under task lighting and magnification for soil, damage, corrosion, function and insulation integrity. Return soiled devices for recleaning and remove faulty devices from use. Assemble hinged instruments unlocked and limit set mass to 7 kg [15.4 lb]. Package in sterile barrier systems (SBSs) conforming to ISO 11607-1 and the relevant part of EN 868, label before sterilisation, and use tamper-evident closures. Validate sealing, wrapping and container closure processes to ISO 11607-2. For heat sealers without a process record, record the set temperature daily and confirm the seal temperature before each sealing episode. Check a sample of seals daily before and after sterilisation. Inspect rigid containers before each use for dents, cracks, gasket condition, latch function and filter status. Maintain the inspection and packing area at positive pressure to adjoining areas and the decontamination area at negative pressure, with ventilation to AS 1668.2.

  • Sterilisation: Use moist heat for heat-tolerant devices. Common holding times are 3 min at 134 deg C [273 deg F] and 15 min at 121 deg C [250 deg F], with the equilibration time determined in PQ added. Supply saturated steam with a dryness value not less than 0.95. Do not sterilise packaged loads in a cycle without a drying phase, and use extended cycles only where the device IFU specifies them. For heat-labile critical and semi-critical devices, use a validated low-temperature process: vaporised hydrogen peroxide to ISO 22441, ethylene oxide to ISO 11135 with in-chamber aeration complete before unloading, low-temperature steam formaldehyde to ISO 25424, or other systems to ISO 14937.

  • Cooling, Storage and Distribution: Leave heat-sterilised loads on the trolley in a dedicated cooling area in the unloading zone until cool, clear of surfaces that create condensate. Store sterile stock in a designated area at 18 to 25 deg C [64 to 77 deg F] and 35% to 70% relative humidity, with continuously operating ventilation to AS 1668.2 and positive pressure to adjoining areas. Log temperature and humidity using the building management system or data loggers. Rotate stock first in, first out, and restrict access to trained staff. Distribute in closed or covered carts. Where devices travel between sites, risk-assess the transport procedure and define the conditions under which transported devices are nonconforming.

Equipment and Services Selection

AS 5369 requires total reprocessing time to be included in operational planning and sufficient equipment for the volume and type of devices processed. Base capacity calculations on instrument inventory, demand for devices reused within the same session, average and peak hourly volume, validated cycle times including loading and unloading, and downtime for maintenance, calibration and requalification. Size each equipment group so that peak demand is met with the largest unit out of service. AS 5369 prohibits routine use of immediate-use sterilisation to cover insufficient inventory, so hold enough instrument sets to meet session demand.

Purchasing procedures include risk-based selection criteria, review by a competent person from the reprocessing facility before any loan, trial or purchase, compatibility with existing reprocessing equipment, ARTG inclusion, validated reprocessing instructions to ISO 17664-1 or ISO 17664-2, and acceptance criteria at delivery. Include IQ, OQ, PQ and operator training in the purchase agreement.

  • Washer-Disinfectors: ISO 15883-1 and ISO 15883-2. Specify capacity in DIN trays per cycle, validated cycle times for each programme, load carriers with lumen irrigation ports to suit the device mix, and cycle record output to the tracking system. Specify pass-through units in new or refurbished facilities so that loading occurs on the dirty side and unloading on the clean side. Multi-chamber batch WDs suit continuous high throughput. Cart washers remove manual cleaning of case carts and transport containers.

  • Automated Flexible Endoscope Reprocessors: ISO 15883-4. Specify cycle time per endoscope, endoscopes per cycle, automated leak testing, channel connection and flow monitoring, a self-disinfection cycle, the water filtration arrangement, and a cycle record linked to the endoscope serial number.

  • Steam Sterilisers: EN 285 for large sterilisers (chamber volume of 60 L [15.9 US gal] and above) and EN 13060 for small sterilisers, with processes validated to ISO 17665. Specify vacuum air removal for porous and lumened loads, an air detector, double-door pass-through configuration from the packing area to the sterile store, chamber capacity in sterilisation units (STUs) of 300 x 300 x 600 mm [12 x 12 x 24"] matched to the throughput model, and cycle record output.

  • Low-Temperature Sterilisers: ISO 22441 for vaporised hydrogen peroxide, EN 1422 and ISO 11135 for ethylene oxide, EN 14180 and ISO 25424 for low-temperature steam formaldehyde, and ISO 14937 for other systems. Before purchase, confirm device compatibility, lumen length and diameter limits, load mass limits and validated cycle times against the facility’s list of heat-labile devices. Ethylene oxide requires gas leak detection where the supply is not self-contained within the chamber, and aeration to ISO 11135.

  • Ultrasonic Cleaners, Drying Cabinets and Endoscope Storage Cabinets: AS 2773, AS 5330 and EN 16442 respectively. Specify lumen irrigation on ultrasonic cleaners where lumened devices are processed. Size CESCs for the peak number of endoscopes in storage, with individual channel connections and storage time recording.

  • Heat Sealers: No equipment standard applies. Validate the sealing process to ISO 11607-2 using ISO/TS 16775 guidance, and specify sealers that record process variables for each seal.

  • Water Treatment: Specify treatment, typically reverse osmosis, to deliver final rinse water for instrument WDs and manual cleaning with pH 5.5 to 8.0, conductivity not more than 30 µS/cm at 20 deg C [68 deg F], total hardness not more than 10 mg/L as CaCO3, chloride not more than 10 mg/L, iron not more than 0.2 mg/L, phosphates not more than 0.2 mg/L, silicates not more than 1.0 mg/L, TVC not more than 100 cfu/100 mL and endotoxin not more than 0.25 EU/mL. Final rinse water for endoscope WDs requires TVC not more than 10 cfu/100 mL, no Pseudomonas aeruginosa or atypical mycobacteria detected in 100 mL, and endotoxin not more than 30 EU/mL. Water for pre-cleaning, cleaning and rinses before the final rinse requires hardness not more than 150 mg/L and chloride not more than 120 mg/L. Provide duty and standby pumps, storage sized for simultaneous peak demand, and sampling points at the point of use. Use laboratories accredited to AS ISO/IEC 17025 for testing.

  • Steam Supply: Size steam generation for the simultaneous peak demand of all sterilisers. For a dedicated steam generator, test feedwater at IQ or OQ and annually, with evaporative residue not more than 10 mg/L, conductivity not more than 5 µS/cm at 20 deg C [68 deg F], hardness not more than 2 mg/L as CaCO3, chloride not more than 0.5 mg/L and pH 5 to 7.5. For a non-dedicated supply, test condensate to EN 285 at IQ or OQ and annually. Test steam quality on installation or relocation of a large steriliser or a change to the steam supply, with a dryness value not less than 0.95, non-condensable gases not more than 3.5% by volume and superheat not more than 25 deg C [45 deg F].

  • Electrical Supply and Ventilation: Assess the need for emergency power to ventilation serving sterile stores that supply operating theatres, and to the equipment needed to sustain reprocessing during supply outages.

  • Instrument Tracking: AS 5369 requires traceability of each critical device to the individual steriliser cycle, and of each semi-critical device to the individual HLD cycle, with records of device identification, cycle number, equipment identification and the competent persons accountable for cleaning, loading and release. NSQHS Action 3.17 extends traceability to the patient and procedure, and AS 5369 states that all facilities should be working towards an electronic system. Specify a system that scans sets and endoscopes at each stage, imports cycle records from WDs, AFERs and sterilisers, blocks release until all required records and indicator results are complete, and links each set or endoscope to the patient record. Check any software change that affects equipment operation before use.

Specify service support in the procurement: response times, local spares holding, and maintenance and requalification by competent persons. AS 5369 requires the maximum permitted delay in scheduled maintenance to be specified, and equipment is not returned to use until specified maintenance is complete and documented.

Staff Training and Competency

The competent person accountable for reprocessing holds relevant qualifications and experience, has authority over reprocessing wherever it occurs in the organisation, and supervises day-to-day activity. AS 5369 requires a formal orientation and training program and periodic competency assessment at intervals defined by the facility. Set staffing levels on an evidence-based rationale and review them before significant changes to activity, equipment or processes.

Initial Training: Assess the knowledge of new staff on commencement. The orientation program covers modes of infection transmission, standard and transmission-based precautions, hand hygiene, workplace health and safety including personal protective equipment (PPE), reprocessing activities, documentation, and the quality management system, and concludes with an objective competency assessment. The national qualifications are HLT37015 Certificate III in Sterilisation Services and HLT47015 Certificate IV in Sterilisation Services. Provide a written procedure for each task and supervised practice until competency is demonstrated.

Competency Assessment: Assess competency by direct observation against task checklists for manual cleaning, WD and steriliser loading, packaging and sealing, indicator interpretation, and release. For endoscope reprocessing, the GESA and GENCA guideline requires skills-based assessment at commencement, annually, after an identified breach and when new equipment is introduced. Train staff annually in infection prevention (PPE, hand hygiene and waste disposal) and in occupational exposure procedures for blood and body substances. Retrain staff after any nonconformance attributed to technique.

Supervisors: Train supervisors in nonconformance control, recall, notification under facility policy and root cause analysis. Run recall exercises to test traceability from cycle to patient.

Loan, Trial and Repaired Devices: Process loan, trial and repaired devices on receipt by validated cleaning and further processing to the IFU. Before sending devices off site, process them by validated cleaning and HLD as a minimum. Test loaned flexible endoscopes, and endoscopes returned from repair, for microbiological contamination within 72 h of receipt. Agree delivery lead times with suppliers so that loan sets arrive with enough time for full reprocessing.

Workplace Safety: Conduct ergonomic risk assessments for sinks and workstations and provide height-adjustable sinks and benches. Train staff in chemical handling using current safety data sheets, reviewed at least annually.

Technology and Process Improvement

  • Automation and Robotics: Automated WD loading and unloading, conveyor-fed WDs and sterilisers, automated guided vehicles and automated storage and retrieval systems reduce manual handling and transit time. Include automated load handling in IQ and OQ, validate load carriers as part of each load configuration, confirm that automated storage meets sterile store temperature, humidity and ventilation requirements, and define a manual fallback procedure for system failure.

  • Equipment and Environment Monitoring: Connect WDs, AFERs and sterilisers to a monitoring system that reports cycle status, cycle failures and fault codes. Machine-level monitoring detects a utility failure only when the machine raises a corresponding fault, so instrument water treatment (conductivity, flow and pressure), steam supply and compressed air separately where continuous assurance is required. Log sterile store temperature and humidity continuously with alarm limits.

  • Cleaning Verification: Visual inspection alone may not confirm cleanliness of complex devices. Use protein residue tests, adenosine triphosphate (ATP) tests or surrogate test devices to audit cleaning. Test soils and surrogate devices used for routine monitoring must be demonstrated equivalent to the test soil used in cleaning validation. ATP measures organic residue, not microbial viability, so use it as a cleaning indicator only and set pass thresholds from facility baseline data. Use borescopes to inspect endoscope channels and long lumens.

  • Endoscope Risk Reduction: Sterilise semi-critical endoscopes wherever they are compatible with a validated low-temperature process, as the AS 5369 hierarchy requires. For duodenoscopes, concentrate controls on distal tip cleaning, drying and storage. Where a device cannot be cleaned to its validated instructions, AS 5369 prohibits reprocessing it and a sterile single-use device is the alternative. Assess single-use endoscopes for high-risk or low-frequency procedures against cost and waste.

  • Facility and Workflow Design: Plan new or refurbished units to AusHFG Part B (0190). Centralise reprocessing where practicable. Point-of-use reprocessing rooms, e.g. in endoscopy units, must meet the same requirements for environmental control, segregation of clean and dirty activities, and unidirectional flow. Locate the CSSD to minimise transit distance to theatres, with separate clean and dirty routes. Apply lean methods to remove waiting and backtracking, e.g. prioritising urgent sets through cleaning, inspection and sterilisation using tracking-system flags.

  • Outsourced and Shared Reprocessing: Off-site reprocessing facilities provide overflow and contingency capacity during equipment failure or refurbishment. Define responsibilities and conformance with AS 5369 in the contract, risk-assess inter-site transport, and require cycle and traceability records to be returned with each consignment.

  • Performance Data: Use tracking and cycle data to measure turnaround time per set, equipment utilisation, queue length, repair frequency per instrument and missing-item rates. Use these measures to set instrument inventory and staffing against the theatre schedule, and to identify instrument designs that repeatedly fail inspection for review with clinical users. Audit the quality system at planned intervals and track corrective actions to closure.

Conclusion

High-volume reprocessing of complex instruments and endoscopes is controlled by compliance with AS 5369:2023 and NSQHS Action 3.17, validated and routinely monitored processes, equipment and services sized for peak demand, competency-assessed staff, and electronic traceability to the patient. Close the gaps identified in the AS 5369 gap analysis against the compliance plan, and requalify every process annually.
