Evaluation of Chemical Indicators for Autoclaves and Washer-Disinfectors

Introduction

A chemical indicator (CI) is a test system that reveals change in one or more pre-specified process variables through a chemical or physical change on exposure to a process. A CI gives an immediate result at item or load level. It does not demonstrate sterility or microbial inactivation.

This paper sets out the indicator types defined for sterilisation processes, their performance limits in routine use, the cost of typical monitoring programs and the indicators used to monitor washer-disinfectors. Requirements are stated in accordance with AS 5369:2023 and the ISO standards it calls up. The analysis is model and manufacturer neutral.

Regulatory and Standards Context in Australia

AS 5369:2023, Reprocessing of reusable medical devices and other devices in health and non-health related facilities, supersedes AS/NZS 4187:2014 and AS/NZS 4815:2006. It is the standard used to implement Action 3.17 of the National Safety and Quality Health Service (NSQHS) Standards. Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care Advisory AS24/01 requires each health service organisation to complete a gap analysis against AS 5369:2023 and to follow a documented compliance plan with its own timeframes. An organisation without a completed gap analysis, or not following its plan, is rated not met. An exemption is required where the planned timeframe exceeds three years.

AS 5369:2023 makes the cycle record the primary basis for release. Check the process record at the end of every sterilisation cycle against the validated specification. CIs and biological indicators (BIs) are additional methods of demonstrating that the process was delivered. For moist heat sterilisation, AS 5369:2023 requires the following:

  • A CI conforming to ISO 11140-1 on the exterior of every packaged item.

  • A CI in every load in which semi-critical items are sterilised unwrapped.

  • Internal CIs as required by facility policy. Where internal CIs are used routinely, include them in performance qualification (PQ).

  • Selection, use and interpretation of CIs and process challenge devices (PCDs) in accordance with ISO 15882.

  • A daily air removal and steam penetration test, before use, on every steriliser that uses vacuum air removal. Bowie and Dick type tests on large sterilisers conform to ISO 11140-3, ISO 11140-4 or ISO 11140-5. Air removal tests on small sterilisers conform to ISO 11140-6.

  • A weekly leak rate test and air detector function test where an air detector is fitted. Where no air detector is fitted, a daily leak rate test before the Bowie and Dick type test.

  • BIs conforming to the ISO 11138 series at a frequency set by the facility, and in every ethylene oxide load.

Do not use a Bowie and Dick type test on sterilisers that use gravity or positive pressure pulse air displacement. Small sterilisers that do not process packaged or hollow items (Type N and some Type S cycles) may not require an air removal test. Where a pre-vacuum steriliser has no air detector, use a suitable PCD to confirm the absence of residual air in the load.

AS 5369:2023 leaves BI frequency for moist heat to the facility. Its informative risk assessment example uses a BI in a PCD at least weekly, preferably daily, and in every implant load, with implant loads quarantined until the BI result is known. Where facility policy makes a BI mandatory for a load, release is delayed until the result is known.

Release of a load from moist heat sterilisation with a drying cycle requires all of the following: the cycle record confirms the parameters established during PQ, external CIs show the specified colour change, packages are intact with no visible moisture, and BI, PCD and electronic data logger results are correct where used.

Chemical Indicator Types (Types 1 to 6)

ISO 11140-1:2014 defines six indicator types. The superseded 2005 edition used the term class, which remains common in trade use. ISO 11140-1 states that the categorisation has no hierarchical significance. The type number defines intended use and performance requirements, not a ranking. For steam, ISO 11140-1 lists time, temperature and moisture as the critical process variables. Verify air removal by the daily air removal test, an air detector or a PCD, not by pack indicators alone.

Type 1: Process Indicator

Function: Shows that an item has been directly exposed to the process and distinguishes processed from unprocessed items. Formats include indicator tape and indicator inks printed on pouches and labels.

Application: Fit to the exterior of every packaged item. Check for the specified colour change before release.

Limitations: Can reach its endpoint at exposures shorter than a sterilising hold. A changed Type 1 indicator confirms exposure only.

Type 2: Indicator for Specific Tests

Function: Used in specified test procedures, principally the Bowie and Dick type air removal and steam penetration test for porous loads, and hollow load tests (helix, fibrous layer or closed-ended tube devices) for small sterilisers.

Application: Run daily before the first load, in an empty chamber after a warm-up cycle, on every steriliser that uses vacuum air removal. A uniform colour change across the indicator is a pass. A paler or non-uniform area indicates residual air, an air leak or non-condensable gas in the steam supply. Remove the steriliser from service on a fail.

Limitations: Tests air removal and steam penetration only. It does not monitor the sterilising hold or the load. Some loads present a greater challenge than the Bowie and Dick type test, so it does not replace load monitoring.

Type 3: Single-Variable Indicator

Function: Reacts to one critical variable at a stated value, e.g. a melt pellet that responds at a set temperature.

Application: Confirms that one variable reached its stated value at one location.

Limitations: Does not respond to time at temperature or to moisture. Do not use as the internal indicator for steam pack monitoring.

Type 4: Multi-Variable Indicator

Function: Reacts to two or more critical variables at stated values, e.g. time and temperature.

Application: Internal pack indicator where facility policy requires one and correlation to BI performance is not required.

Limitations: Not required to respond to all three steam variables and not correlated to BI performance. A fail does not identify which variable was deficient.

Type 5: Integrating Indicator

Function: Reacts to all critical variables. Stated values are generated to be equivalent to, or exceed, the performance requirements for BIs in the ISO 11138 series over a range of temperatures. The common format is a moving-front strip read against an accept zone.

Application: Internal pack indicator at the most difficult-to-sterilise location, or inside a PCD as a per-cycle load monitor. One Type 5 indicator covers several cycle temperatures within its stated range.

Limitations: Does not detect microbial inactivation and does not replace a BI where facility policy or the process requires one. Unit cost is higher than Types 1 and 4.

Type 6: Emulating Indicator

Function: Reacts to all critical variables for a specified cycle, e.g. 134 deg C [273 deg F] for 3 min. ISO 11140-1 sets the fail condition at 6 % less time and 1 deg C [1.8 deg F] less temperature than the stated values.

Application: Select only where the stated values match the validated cycle. Common saturated steam holding times are 121 deg C [250 deg F] for 15 min, 126 deg C [259 deg F] for 10 min, 132 deg C [270 deg F] for 4 min and 134 deg C [273 deg F] for 3 min.

Limitations: Valid only for the cycle it emulates. A facility running several cycle types holds separate stock for each. A 2020 evaluation recorded Type 6 indicators reaching endpoint in steam cycles that the steriliser record identified as failed. Where an indicator result conflicts with the cycle record, the release criteria are not met. Quarantine the load.

Laboratory Versus Field Performance

Each indicator is manufactured to reach its endpoint at stated values under reference conditions in a test vessel with controlled steam. In routine use the result also depends on placement, steam quality, interpretation, storage and steriliser condition. Control each factor as follows.

Placement: Place internal indicators at the location validation identified as most difficult to sterilise. A PCD used for routine monitoring must be equivalent to, or more challenging than, that location. No standard currently specifies PCDs for routine monitoring of large steam sterilisers, so demonstrate equivalence during PQ.

Steam quality: AS 5369:2023 requires the measured chamber temperature and the saturated steam temperature calculated from chamber pressure to remain within the sterilisation temperature band throughout the holding time, with a minimum steam dryness value of 0.95. Superheated steam, wet steam and air and steam mixtures cause sterilisation failure while the chamber temperature can read within limits. Indicator response under these conditions depends on indicator design. Confirm steam quality from the temperature and pressure correlation on each cycle record and by steam quality tests at requalification, not from indicator results.

Interpretation: Read every indicator at unloading against the endpoint reference in the instructions for use (IFU) and record the result in the cycle record. Treat any incomplete or ambiguous change as a fail. Use indicators within their expiry date and store them to the IFU temperature and humidity limits.

Failure response: On any failed or suspect result, quarantine the load, assess items released since the last acceptable result for recall, and investigate. The AS 5369:2023 risk assessment example returns a steriliser to service after three consecutive acceptable Bowie and Dick type tests and three acceptable BIs in a PCD.

Steriliser condition: Indicator monitoring does not replace equipment qualification. Complete annual requalification, including thermometric testing, and calibrate the steriliser temperature and pressure instruments so that the cycle record remains a valid basis for release.

Cost of Routine Monitoring

Annual indicator cost is set by the daily air removal test, the number of internal indicators and the BI frequency. External Type 1 indicators are a minor cost. This model uses stated assumptions. Replace the unit prices with contract prices and the throughput with site data before use.

Assumptions: One large pre-vacuum steam steriliser running 5 cycles per day with 20 packaged items per cycle, on 260 operating days per year. This gives 1,300 cycles and 26,000 packaged items per year. Unit prices are Australian distributor list prices including GST, checked in October 2026 for single-box purchases.

Unit prices:

  • Type 1 process indicator (tape or printed pouch indicator): AUD $0.01 to $0.05 per item.

  • Type 2 disposable Bowie and Dick type test pack: AUD $8 to $16 per test.

  • Type 4 indicator strip: AUD $0.10 to $0.30 each.

  • Type 5 or Type 6 indicator strip: AUD $0.40 to $0.80 each.

  • Self-contained BI: AUD $7 to $20 each, increasing as readout time falls from 24 h to 24 min. Incubator or reader capital cost excluded.

  • Washer-disinfector test soil indicator: approximately AUD $3 each. Holder excluded.

Annual cost per steriliser:

  • Type 1 on every packaged item: AUD $260 to $1,300.

  • Daily Bowie and Dick type test: AUD $2,080 to $4,160.

  • One Type 5 per cycle, in a PCD or the most difficult pack: AUD $520 to $1,040.

  • Type 4 in every pack: AUD $2,600 to $7,800.

  • Type 5 in every pack: AUD $10,400 to $20,800.

  • Weekly BI, including one positive control per test: AUD $728 to $2,080.

  • Daily BI, including one positive control per test: AUD $3,640 to $10,400.

A program of Type 1 on every item, a daily Bowie and Dick type test, one Type 5 per cycle and a weekly BI costs AUD $3,588 to $8,580 per steriliser per year. The daily air removal test is the largest fixed cost. Internal indicator density and BI frequency are the largest variable costs. Placing a Type 5 in every pack instead of one per cycle multiplies the internal indicator count by 20 and adds AUD $9,880 to $19,760 per year. Moving from weekly to daily BIs adds AUD $2,912 to $8,320 per year. Set both from the facility risk assessment and the PQ result.

Where a reusable electronic air removal test device is considered, confirm it conforms to ISO 11140-3, ISO 11140-4 or ISO 11140-5 and compare its capital and calibration cost with the disposable test cost over the device life. For a washer-disinfector, a daily test soil indicator costs approximately AUD $780 per year at 260 operating days. Testing every cycle multiplies this by the number of cycles per day. Include holders in the first-year cost.

Procure only CIs that conform to the ISO 11140 part for the process, and test soils demonstrated as equivalent to the test soil used in cleaning validation.

Chemical Indicators in Washer-Disinfectors

The ISO 11140 types apply to sterilisation. Washer-disinfector (WD) monitoring uses separate indicators for cleaning efficacy, thermal disinfection and chemical disinfection. AS 5369:2023 requires WD PQ to the relevant part of the ISO 15883 series and confirmation of the effectiveness of each reprocessing phase before release to the next. Residual soil compromises disinfection and sterilisation, so a failed cleaning result stops the item at the cleaning stage.

Cycle verification: Release from automated cleaning and thermal disinfection requires each item to be visually clean and dry and the cycle record to conform to the process specification. At the end of each cycle, confirm that water pressure, flow and spray action, cleaning agent dosage, disinfection temperature and holding time were within validated limits. Inspect each item under magnification where required.

Cleaning efficacy indicators: A test soil indicator carries a standardised soil on a carrier placed in the load. It provides a reproducible challenge where visual inspection of complex devices is not sufficient. AS 5369:2023 states that commercial test soils may be used provided they have been demonstrated as equivalent to the test soil used in cleaning validation. ISO 15883-5 specifies the test soils and methods. The AS 5369:2023 risk assessment example runs a WD verification test at least weekly, preferably daily. Place the indicator at the load position validation identified as hardest to clean. Any residual soil is a fail. Remove the WD from service, check spray arm rotation, nozzles, filters, pump pressure, detergent dosing and water temperature, then retest before return to service.

Thermal disinfection: A0 is the equivalent time in seconds at 80 deg C [176 deg F] with a z value of 10 deg C [18 deg F]. AS 5369:2023 lists surface temperature and minimum holding time combinations that achieve A0 600: 70 deg C [158 deg F] for 100 min, 75 deg C [167 deg F] for 30 min, 80 deg C [176 deg F] for 10 min and 90 deg C [194 deg F] for 1 min. Confirm the A0 required for the intended use against ISO 15883-1. Verify each cycle from the WD process record. Irreversible temperature labels confirm only that a surface exceeded a set temperature. They do not record holding time and cannot confirm A0. Use them only as a supplementary check at load positions.

Chemical disinfection: For endoscope WDs and manual high-level disinfection, the controlling parameter is the minimum recommended concentration (MRC), the lowest concentration specified for use in the process. Test the MRC with a test strip compatible with the disinfectant before each use as the IFU specifies, or at least daily. Where the solution is reused, test immediately before each use, since reuse dilutes it. Where the IFU requires, run positive and negative controls on opening each new strip container to confirm the strips detect a concentration below the MRC. Discard the solution on a fail. Confirm temperature, contact time and rinse water volume for each use. Record the disinfectant and strip batch numbers, expiry and opening dates, control results, MRC results and the competent person performing each check.

Water quality and ancillary tests: For WDs to ISO 15883-1 and ISO 15883-2, AS 5369:2023 sets supply water hardness and chloride testing monthly, final rinse conductivity monthly, final rinse total viable count (TVC) monthly for the first 12 months then reducible to annually while results remain within specification, final rinse endotoxin annually, and a volumetric chemical dosing test quarterly. For endoscope WDs to ISO 15883-4, test final rinse water monthly for TVC, Pseudomonas aeruginosa and atypical Mycobacterium species, sampled where the endoscope connects to the WD. Adjust frequencies with results and increase them when a water specification is exceeded. Test ultrasonic cleaners daily to AS 2773.

Conclusion

Under AS 5369:2023 the cycle record is the primary release criterion. CIs confirm exposure at item and load level, and BIs provide microbiological confirmation at a frequency set by the facility. The minimum steam requirement is a CI on every packaged item, a CI in every unwrapped semi-critical load and a daily air removal test on vacuum sterilisers. Select internal indicators by function. Use Type 5 where one indicator must cover several cycles and correlate to BI performance, Type 6 only where its stated values match a single validated cycle, and Type 4 where neither is required. Set internal indicator density and BI frequency from the facility risk assessment.

For WDs, verify each cycle from the process record, inspect each item, confirm cleaning efficacy with a test soil equivalent to the validation soil and test the MRC before each use of a chemical disinfectant. Procure CIs conforming to the applicable part of ISO 11140, select and interpret them to ISO 15882, and record every result.
